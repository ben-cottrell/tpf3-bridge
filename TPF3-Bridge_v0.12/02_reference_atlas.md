# Passenger railway reference atlas

> **Historical/reference chapter.** The [v0.12 implementation specification](IMPLEMENTATION_SPEC.md) governs current scope and supersedes earlier next-step priorities. Detailed physics and further station-internal simulation are optional deferred work; the findings below retain their original scope.

Version **0.2.0** · 20 September 2026 · **25 reference records**

This is a research and pattern-selection atlas, not a collection of completed replicas. Records R01–R23 have reviewed primary material relevant to inventory, operation, circulation or a project; R24–R25 are preliminary station/access records. None has a complete imported, dated interlocking and measured approach alignment. See [evidence levels](01_scope_and_evidence.md).

## 3. What the expanded sample adds

The sample is deliberately not a ranking of the best stations. It covers different engineering problems: very large terminal banks; group-separated operations; mixed through/terminal stations; longitudinal platform sections; separate vertical rail layers; complex corridor junctions; and pedestrian interchange across many tracks.

For generation, select patterns by required function and constraints. A 20-label reference should not automatically inspire a 20-road generic station. Conversely, a smaller reference can expose a difficult bottleneck that a larger station does not contain.

## 5. Acquisition order

Obtain technical route/track evidence first for Birmingham/Proof House, Clapham and Willesden because they are explicit user priorities and stress different parts of the model. Follow with Waterloo, Victoria, Euston and the King's Cross approaches. Then use Leeds and Edinburgh to verify the berth/alias design, and Reading/London Bridge to test corridor-scale intervention.

Do not postpone the Python engine until every site is surveyed: synthetic, explicitly labelled fixtures can test the model now. Do postpone claims of exact reconstruction until the evidence gates are met.
