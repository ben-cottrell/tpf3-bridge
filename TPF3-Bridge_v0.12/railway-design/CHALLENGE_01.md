# Process test: oblique four-lead terminal throat

Prepared 9 October 2026. Initial fixture now built with all eight native directed
paths verified; see [the prospective design record](examples/challenge01/DESIGN.md)
and its plan/as-built overlay. User visual feedback and a materially changed
transfer variant remain outstanding. The original brief follows.
This advances beyond copying or mirroring the supplied Shefford fans.

## Purpose and input fixture

Design a compact two-directional-track approach serving four terminal-like leads,
on open level terrain. The outputs are laterally displaced and angled relative to
the input. The whole throat must be planned before native fitting.

Proposed reproducible local-coordinate fixture:
- Approach axis +x; input tracks 5units apart, UP toward the terminal and DOWN away.
  The approach mouths are (0,-2.5) and (0,+2.5); straight stubs extend toward -x.
- Centre of the four receiving mouths: (220,35), with downstream axis +15degrees
  relative to +x. Offset each mouth along the normal to that axis by -12.5,-2.5,
  +2.5,+12.5units: track gaps10/5/10, allowing platform space in two gaps.
- Terminal-like straight stubs extend downstream along the receiving axis. No
  station building, train purchase, terrain structures or signalling-capacity
  experiment is necessary for the initial geometry/design test.
- Coordinates are proposed experimental inputs, not inferred native limits or a
  guaranteed fit. Before use, translate the fixture to a surveyed clear map area
  and confirm lead roles. Treat a fixture revision explicitly, not as an invisible
  means of making a failed design pass.

Required movement contract: UP reaches each of the four leads; each lead reaches
DOWN. Reversal at a terminal is intentional; a reversing manoeuvre inside the throat
is not. Preserve through approach tracks and use ordinary turnouts/single crossovers
where useful. Scissors and extra pointwork solely for unrequested choices are not
design goals. All eight movements need a place in the initial plan.

## Work before fitting

1. Draw the entire scaled arrangement: route families, choice/merge order, curved
   boundaries, crossover regions and reserved space. Include the functional return
   routes from the start. Choose the footprint and explain the trade-offs.
2. Compare alternative arrangements only where consequential. Explain which route
   controls space, which branch should be built first, and how later routes fit.
3. Save the selected revision and its predicted difficult attachment regions before
   native proposals. Do not retrospectively call the final result the original plan.
4. Fit/build the controlling family, then the remaining group, under the active
   construction authority. Minor adjustments may proceed; material redesign follows
   the procedure. Preserve the manual reference examples during this test.

## Assessment

- Function: all eight native directed paths; report actual train operation separately
  if later included. A simple undirected graph is insufficient.
- Shape: coherent related curves, compact placement of exchanges, no compensating
  loop, avoidable detour or late kink; justify any material deviation.
- Process: final overlay against the saved plan; remaining space was accounted for;
  compare major replans/rebuilds and human corrections with what caused them. Native
  discoveries can legitimately require redesign; no arbitrary zero-rework target.
- Scope: native failure alone is not a bridge bug; no new radius/clearance gates or
  capacity investigation to distract from the design outcome.

Keep measurements comparable at matched inputs/outputs. User visual review provides
a separate quality judgement after the designer has made its own assessment. A compact
result cannot compensate for missing movements. Passing one example does not establish
general design reliability.

## Transfer and adaptation after the first result

Use the same eight-movement contract with a materially changed receiving direction
or offset, or a user-marked limited footprint. Select that variation after the initial
plan is recorded. Predict the changed arrangement before fitting. Do not count a rigid
translation, rotation or mirror as a strong transfer test.

Assess whether the designer reuses relationships appropriately while revising junction
order or corridors when necessary. A change introduced after construction is a separate
adaptation test; do not label its resulting legitimate rework a planning failure.
Grade separation belongs in a later small test of crossing order and profiles, so a
failure here remains attributable to horizontal topology/geometry and build sequence.
