## Selected whole arrangement

See plan-r1.svg and plan-r1.json, saved before fitting.
Two through tracks run to the inner receiving leads L2/L3. Their related sweeps
start near x110. Two outer branches create L1/L4 near x125–145. Outer branches
are fitted before any optional detail; there is no intermediate third branch to
trap between them in this two-output family.

An arrival crossover spans approximately x10–50 from UP to the other spine.
A separate return crossover spans x60–100 from the DOWN spine to the UP spine
when described in increasing x; reverse travel gives L1/L2 access to DOWN.
Both exchanges precede both fans. Four ordinary crossover turnouts and two fan
turnouts serve the complete contract. No scissors, diamond or throat reversal.

Rejected alternative: merge the approach pair into one common four-lead fan.
It reduces the number of crossovers but introduces a common single-track section
and loses the two continuous approach corridors. Scissors would shorten the
exchange but conflict with the brief's preference for separate single crossovers.

## Sequence and uncertainties

1. Place all six boundary stubs, then the two straight approaches/sweeps as a pair.
2. Trial the two crossover attachment regions while they remain unobstructed.
3. Fit the outer L4 fan boundary, then L1, retaining the inner pair's reservations.
4. Check all eight directed native TRAIN paths and overlay actual geometry on R1.

Uncertain: native turnout spacing in the short central exchange; through-track
replacement around the curve onset; coexistence of the two closely related curves.
Native proposals decide acceptance. Shift points within their intended region or
adjust controls locally; a footprint/route-order change requires R2 first.
Radius is feedback, not a target or invented rejection gate. The two oblique fans
need to arrive tangent to the15° receiving leads; a smooth change of curvature
can be legitimate, but a compensating loop or reversal cannot.

## Outcome

Built on the unchanged fixture. All eight directed native TRAIN paths pass:
UP to each lead and each lead to DOWN. Native route lengths including the boundary
stubs are 325.47–327.60 units. Twenty track edges and six ordinary three-way nodes
form the layout. No train operation or simultaneous-movement capacity is claimed.

All construction proposals were accepted on their first selected candidate. No
rebuild, material replan or human layout correction was needed. The user activated
the adapter and supplied a signal on the separate bootstrap track; this is not a
claim of completely unaided setup. Existing bridge capabilities sufficed.

The built-overlay.svg/png compares the original orange R1 centreline with native
BaseEdge geometry in navy. Fitted control lengths vary slightly; the planned
crossover regions, fan order, footprint and boundary conditions remain unchanged.
These centreline curves do not expose internal native turnout movement geometry.
Raw proposal/path receipts remain in .local_runs/design/challenge01/; result.json
contains the compact outcome and captured geometry.

Designer assessment: the paired sweeps and ordinary fan branches remain coherent,
with no compensating loop or late detour. The crossovers occupy the straight
approach and preserve both through corridors. This is a satisfactory first
prospective composition, pending the user's visual judgement. It is not evidence
of general reliability or proof that 220 units is the smallest practical footprint.

The useful lesson is to reserve both arrival and return choices before fitting
either fan. Designing the complete family made the local native fits routine in
this case. The next transfer test should materially change the receiving heading
or lateral offset, recording its spatial arrangement before fitting; merely
mirroring this result would provide weak additional evidence.
