# Reusable railway patterns

Apply these relationships using a fresh site survey. Numeric examples are preferences
or measured references, not native minima. See the [handbook](../RAILWAY_DESIGN_GUIDE.md)
for fitting, terrain and operation details.

## F1 — Fan boundaries before intermediate tracks

Establish the controlling outer routes at the end of the straight approach. Reserve
space for intermediates and join them to the parent with compatible curvature.
Build the route most likely to be trapped first. Remove a constraining intermediate
connection when necessary instead of forcing an awkward remaining route.

## F2 — Compact staggered three-exit fan

Keep one straight route; branch towards the outermost lead, then split the middle
lead from that curve near the first turnout wherever native construction permits.
Two ordinary turnouts avoid reliance on a single three-way point. Fit the entire
family to preserve smooth turning. Track-only 5/10 offsets differ from station
10/15 offsets allowing a platform; neither dictates longitudinal point spacing.
See the reusable [Shefford geometry](examples/SHEFFORD_FANS.md) and
[track-only comparison](examples/SHEFFORD_TRACK_ONLY.md).

## X1 — Compact single-crossover groups

Retain the required through roads. Arrange separate single crossovers in travel order
so each directed route can use its next exchange without reversal. Parallel strips
may overlap longitudinally; shared-rail junctions need coordinated spacing. Do not
add scissors or route choices the brief does not need. Native acceptance decides
local fit; there is no universal crossover length or point gap.

## J1 — Fork placement shapes the crossing

Compare forks before and after the crossing when planning the whole junction.
Splitting a slow road earlier may permit several upper routes over a lower pair in
one compact zone. Prioritise mainline corridors and relocate secondary structures
when appropriate, while reserving every required branch's space.

## S1 — Native structure sequence

A grouped bridge can become buildable after clearing affected parallel track,
preparing terrain, building/reconnecting the shared upper structure, then constructing
the lower route. Other sites need the lower route first. Treat sequence as a design
choice, not a universal recipe. Rebuild parent rails through supported operations;
do not delete generated model objects independently. Apply site-specific terrain floors.

## O1 — Shared platform choice after the common merge

If B and C arrivals both need either platform, their merge must precede the shared
platform-choice turnout in the arrival direction. A turnout on B before that merge
cannot serve C. Correct source ordering before appending a compensating feeder.
Check arrival, return and alternative-platform routes, bindings and signal direction.
Infrastructure connectivity alone does not establish station service access.

## G3 — Shallow level flyover with earth approaches

Choose the crossing and landing corridors first. Start with a shallow 10–15 degree
level span, ending roughly 5–10 units laterally beyond the crossed outer rails,
then use NORMAL earth-supported ramps where suitable. These user preferences trade
length for a narrower footprint. Build the controlling span first when useful.
Assess both ramps, paired geometry, abutments, native clearance and the whole outline.
A level turnout run-in may resolve vertical fitting rejection without lowering a
whole-route grade target.

## D4 — A station district as connected route families

Reserve platform fans, reorders, normal running order, regional branches and receiving
interfaces together. Use one direction frame on both station sides. Include depot
footprints/open sides and urban/road space. Keep the fast pair aligned and gather
slow and independent Cross tracks into a narrow six-track approach where requested.
Use roughly 30 m straight platform leads, appropriately slow compact fans and
crossovers that do not unnecessarily reduce the fan approach speed. Build pointwork
before signals, place decisions before platform choices, and provide ordinary blocks
on uninterrupted lines. For the real mega-city map, use the handbook's 1.2 km/3 km
flyover/regional-junction planning preferences rather than demonstration spacing.

## Learning from manual examples

A useful reference states approach direction, intended connections and why its shape
works. Inspect fresh native geometry, elevations and topology; do not guess missing
feedback. A mirrored or changed receiving layout tests transfer better than replaying
identical coordinates. Keep distilled geometry/patterns as reusable inputs; discard
inspection logs and activity records after extracting the lesson.
