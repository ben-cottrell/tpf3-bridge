# Design regression cases

Purpose: catch recurrence of demonstrated design mistakes. This is a small reasoning
exercise, not another engine test suite. No live mutation is required for replay.

For a changed procedure, select cases affected by that change. Give the evaluator
only the case's Situation/Task and relevant raw inputs plus the candidate procedure;
retain the Review criteria for assessment afterward. Preserve the resulting plan,
its procedure version, evidence and assessment. Do not claim independence when the
author evaluates its own work or has read the expected decisions.

Assess required functions, spatial coherence, native adaptability and whether major
changes are correctly escalated back to design. Mark each concern addressed, missed
or unknown, with a reason. Alternative solutions pass if justified; exact geometry,
wording and resemblance to one drawing are not requirements.

## R1 — A constrained fan

**Situation:** one straight approach must serve three adjacent platform leads.
The outer connections define the available space; the middle lead can branch from
either outer route. All prior experimental tracks are disposable.
**Task:** propose the arrangement, construction order and response if an initially
built middle connection blocks an outer route. No coordinates are supplied: produce
a relational concept and specify the survey needed before a scaled build plan.

**Review criteria:** whole family and reserved space; controlling routes first;
compatible sweep/parent selection; willingness to remove constraining track;
no invented radius, minimum split angle or claimed native feasibility.

## R2 — Locally accepted, globally misplaced return

**Situation:** B and C arrivals merge into one station approach. Both must reach
either platform. A native-accepted turnout is on B before the merge; C cannot reach
the second platform. An extra long feeder would connect C to that turnout.
**Task:** decide the next design action and explain how to verify the required result.

**Review criteria:** diagnose order, consider common post-merge source, compare
replacement before adding feeder, include all relevant arrival/departure functions.
An accepted track proposal is not proof of the complete service requirement.

## R3 — Small change versus major workaround

**Situation:** a paired branch has a reserved low crossing corridor. Candidate A
moves its turnout slightly while preserving junction order, pair relationship and
neighbouring space. Candidate B builds successfully by looping outward, using an
extra height level and landing in another route's reserved corridor.
**Task:** classify each change and issue the next construction/design instruction.

**Review criteria:** allow A with proportionate native fitting; B triggers a revised
whole-family plan before dependent build, not automatic acceptance or human approval.
No universal distance tolerance; assess cumulative deviations against selected plan.
If evidence ultimately justifies B, it may be selected through a revised spatial
plan that accommodates the displaced route. The trigger requires design work; it
is not an absolute ban on loops, separated pairs or additional levels.

## R4 — Structure rejection and a manual counterexample

**Situation:** a four-track bridge rejects; a user demonstrates a similar grouped
bridge after removing neighbouring track and preparing terrain. Relative lowering
at this site previously caused water; the brief specifies surface z>=1.
**Task:** propose a useful trial and explain what its outcomes would establish.

**Review criteria:** examine geometry/state and construction sequence, respect the
actual floor, observe resulting surface, avoid classifying rejection alone as a bridge
bug or adopting a universal excavation/clearance number. No speculative host repair.

## R5 — Connectivity succeeds, design quality fails

**Situation:** all required paths pass. The as-built view shows separated paired
routes, an unplanned outward hook and an extra crossing level; the brief preferred
a compact coherent arrangement. Operations are only partly observed.
**Task:** write a completion assessment and the next decision, assuming review-only
scope. Explain what additional evidence is needed to assess a replacement.

**Review criteria:** separate built/connected/operating/design conclusions; recognise
visible design defects without inventing an engine bug; preserve review-only scope;
do not declare optimality or begin repairs. Ask for missing intent only if consequential.

## Transfer exercise and comparison

Once a built reference is available, give the designer a mirrored or altered fan
with fresh coordinates and a changed receiving direction or neighbour constraint.
Require its scaled plan and intended sequence before showing the reference solution
for that variant. Native trials then answer the identified uncertainty under the
active construction brief. This is prospective evidence; historical replay is not.

Compare matched endpoints/region and equivalent required functions. Use only useful
measures: footprint, detour, additional crossings, paired-corridor separation, major
rebuilds and unsolicited user corrections. Include comparable images and explanations.
Correctness cannot be traded away for a smaller footprint. A larger route may be
justified by a demonstrated native constraint. No aggregate score hides that trade-off.

Baseline policy: retain the previous record and procedure version. If an affected
case regresses, revise the procedure or document the deliberate requirement change;
do not silently weaken expectations. P75 is a functional baseline and a source of
counterexamples, not the accepted aesthetic target. A fresh, user-reviewed reference
can establish a design-quality baseline for its stated context.
